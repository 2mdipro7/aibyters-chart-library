# Paid / subscription apps on Frappe Cloud Marketplace and third-party app licensing (state as of Oct 2026)

Research method note: frappe.io, frappecloud.com, docs.frappe.io and discuss.frappe.io were **not reachable from this research environment** (egress proxy refused the hosts), so official-docs and forum content below is quoted from search-engine snippets of those pages, not from full-page reads. Press source code was read directly from a fresh shallow clone of github.com/frappe/press (HEAD commit 1904e38d, dated 2026-10-20), and real-world app code was read from raw.githubusercontent.com. Each finding is tagged [SOURCE CODE], [OFFICIAL DOCS/BLOG via snippet], or [COMMUNITY].

## Key Question 1: What does a paid marketplace plan look like, and what revenue share does Frappe take?

### Takeaway
A paid marketplace app plan is a `Marketplace App Plan` record with a title, `price_inr`, `price_usd`, a monthly interval, a feature-bullet list and version restrictions; customers are billed monthly pro-rata on their Frappe Cloud invoice. Frappe keeps 100% of an app's first USD 500 of revenue, then takes a 20% commission (publisher keeps 80% minus gateway charges), with payouts generated monthly as `Payout Order`s (cash via PayPal/bank, or Frappe Cloud credits).

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL DOCS via snippet] "Frappe Cloud takes the first $500 of the revenue the app makes. After that the payment will be split 80-20, where 80% will go to you and 20% to Frappe Cloud." The docs state these terms took effect 1 April 2023. "For non-free apps, a payout is made to the publisher at the end of every month." "Publishers outside India can currently only be paid via PayPal, and need to raise a PayPal invoice at the end of the month." — [Payouts - Frappe Cloud docs](https://docs.frappe.io/cloud/marketplace/payouts)
- [OFFICIAL BLOG via snippet] Original paid-apps launch post (2022): "No cut until you make your first $500! After that it's a straight 80-20 split... you get to keep 80% of what your app makes minus the payment gateway charges." "You can either take payout as Cash or Frappe Cloud credits." Beta-tested with POSAwesome, ICICI Bank Integration and Tanzania Customizations. "you can create a maximum of 3 plans with a monthly recurring fee"; "We are working on prepaid and one-time free type of plans." — [Grow with Frappe Cloud and Marketplace](https://frappe.io/blog/frappe-cloud/grow-with-frappe-cloud-and-marketplace); same text mirrored in the author's gist — [fc-marketplace-blog.md (NagariaHussain)](https://gist.github.com/NagariaHussain/efbfab7e04b00372cd0cde5964d04a96)
- [OFFICIAL DOCS via snippet] "Currently only monthly recurring plans are available. These are charged on pro-rata basis." Example: a customer on a "$30/mo plan for 10 days" who uninstalls "will be charged $10 for that app at the end of the month." Subscribers are listed "In your marketplace app dashboard, under the subscriptions tab." Customers pick the plan at install time or later from the site dashboard. — [App Pricing Plans - Frappe Cloud docs](https://docs.frappe.io/cloud/app-plan)
- [SOURCE CODE] `Marketplace App Plan` doctype fields: `enabled`, `title`, `app`, `interval` (Select), `price_inr`, `price_usd`, `available_on_versions` (table), `features` (table of `Plan Feature` bullets), `roles` (table). The app's `subscription_type` is auto-derived: both free and paid plans => "Freemium", only paid => "Paid", only free => "Free". — [press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_plan/marketplace_app_plan.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_plan/marketplace_app_plan.py) and [.json](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_plan/marketplace_app_plan.json)
- [SOURCE CODE] Commission logic: `MarketplaceAppPayment.get_commission(total)` returns `total * Press Settings.commission` **only after** `has_threshold_passed()` (cumulative net USD+INR-converted total >= `Press Settings.threshold`); before the threshold the commission equals the whole amount (i.e. Frappe keeps 100%). A code TODO admits partial-threshold months are not pro-rated ("if first month collection: $20, second month: $1000 and $500 - cap/threshold then commission should be calculated for $520"). The actual numeric values (threshold, commission %) live in `Press Settings` on Frappe's production instance and are not in the repo; the docs give them as $500 and 20%. — [press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_payment/marketplace_app_payment.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_payment/marketplace_app_payment.py)
- [SOURCE CODE] Payouts: `create_marketplace_payout_orders_monthly()` groups paid invoice items per app team into a `Payout Order` (status Draft / Paid / Commissioned) with per-row `total_amount`, `commission`, `net_amount`, `gateway_fee`, `currency` (INR or USD); only items from **paid** customer invoices are included ("Invoice {name} is not paid yet"); a cash payout requires a "Frappe Purchase Order" before it can be marked Paid. — [press/press/doctype/payout_order/payout_order.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/press/doctype/payout_order/payout_order.py)
- [SOURCE CODE] Currencies: plans carry both INR and USD prices; payout rows are computed per currency (`row.currency == "INR"` else USD). No other currencies appear in the marketplace billing code. — [payout_order.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/press/doctype/payout_order/payout_order.py)
- [SOURCE CODE] Gating: a team may install a paid app only if `team.can_install_paid_apps()` is true, i.e. it is a free account, a billing team, has a payment mode set, or has at least one paid invoice; otherwise the error is "You cannot install a Paid app on Free Credits. Please buy credits before trying to install again." — [press/api/site.py get_app_subscriptions](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/api/site.py) and [press/press/doctype/team/team.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/press/doctype/team/team.py)
- [SOURCE CODE] Free trials: there is no per-app-plan trial field. Site-level trials exist: `eligible_for_trial()` is true only for a team's first site, and a trial site gets `trial_end_date = today + 14 days`; the SaaS flow (`Saas Settings.billing_type == "prepaid"`) injects the trial expiry into site config. — [press/api/marketplace.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/api/marketplace.py) and [marketplace_app_subscription.py set_keys_in_site_config](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/marketplace/doctype/marketplace_app_subscription/marketplace_app_subscription.py)
- [OFFICIAL DOCS via snippet] Listing rules: "All apps on the Marketplace must be Open Source... MIT or compatible with the GNU General Public License"; app must be hosted on GitHub and owned by the publisher; listing is reviewed and approved. — [Frappe Cloud Marketplace Overview](https://docs.frappe.io/cloud/marketplace)
- [OFFICIAL BLOG via snippet] Separate program for **free** apps (announced Oct 2023): Frappe shares "25% of our top-line hosting revenue with all app developers on the Frappe Cloud marketplace", weighted by a 1–10 rating assigned by Frappe (complexity, dependencies, marketing effort); developers must opt in and be approved. — [Revenue sharing with 3rd party apps](https://frappe.io/blog/announcements/revenue-sharing-with-3rd-party-apps-on-frappe-cloud-marketplace); third-party summary claiming quarterly payouts (unconfirmed by the official post) — [Blackslate](https://www.blackslate.io/news/frappe-cloud-revenue-sharing-program-for-free-and-open-source-apps)
- [COMMUNITY] A Sept/Oct 2026 forum post restates the terms as "Marketplace apps must be open source, including paid apps" and "Frappe Cloud takes the first $500... then 80/20", and asks what sustainable model remains for standalone commercial products (paid open source, support contracts, hosted services, private distribution outside Marketplace). — [Genuine question: intended business model for independent Frappe app developers](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/genuine-question-what-is-the-intended-business-model-for-independent-frappe-app-developers/164881)
- [COMMUNITY / OFFICIAL] The "new marketplace" (Sept 2026): the registry now lives in GitHub repo frappe/marketplace (`apps.json` index + `apps/<name>.json` release lists pinned to branch+commit), consumed by `bench get-app` and the Pilot server-manager UI; submissions are PRs with automated Semgrep + install checks replacing manual approval; "new apps on Frappe Cloud v2 are published from there." The README contains **no mention of paid apps, plans or pricing**. — [frappe/marketplace README](https://github.com/frappe/marketplace); [The New Marketplace (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/the-new-marketplace/164661); [Pilot (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/pilot-a-new-bench-with-admin-ui/162974); [frappe/pilot](https://github.com/frappe/pilot)

### Inferences
- The economic shape is: Frappe is a payment processor + distribution channel taking 20% after a $500 lifetime deductible; the publisher never touches the customer's card (customers pay their regular Frappe Cloud invoice).
- Because the $500 threshold is cumulative per app (`Marketplace App Payment.total_usd/total_inr`), a low-priced app may run many months before its first payout.
- The press code still contains the full paid-plan machinery as of Oct 2026, and the registry README is silent on pricing, so the Frappe-Cloud-v2 / frappe-marketplace transition appears to keep billing inside Frappe Cloud (press) while moving app *discovery/submission* to GitHub. This is an inference; no official statement on paid plans in the new registry was found.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the "max 3 plans" cap from the 2022 launch post still applies (the code has no hard-coded cap).
- Could not read the full Payouts docs page (host blocked); supported payout countries beyond "India vs non-India (PayPal)" are unknown.
- No official statement found on whether/how paid plans work for apps submitted through the new frappe/marketplace registry or on Frappe Cloud v2.

## Key Question 2: Which config keys / APIs let an app detect its plan inside the site, and what is the recommended pattern?

### Takeaway
Frappe Cloud writes a per-site, per-app secret into the site's `site_config.json` under the key `sk_<app_name>` (plus, in the SaaS flow, a `subscription` JSON object); the app reads it via `frappe.conf.sk_<app>` and POSTs it to the guest-accessible endpoints `press.api.developer.marketplace.get_subscription_status` (returns the status string) or `get_subscription_info` (returns `{document_name, enabled, plan, site}`) on frappecloud.com. The plan name is **not** written locally; it must be fetched. Real apps (Frappe HR, Insights forks, third-party apps) wrap this in a cached helper and degrade gracefully when the key is absent (self-hosted).

### Cited Findings

- [SOURCE CODE] Injection at site creation: `press.api.site.new()` builds `secret_keys = {f"sk_{s.document_name}": s.secret_key for s in subscription_docs}` and calls `site._update_configuration(secret_keys)` before insert. — [press/api/site.py](https://github.com/frappe/press/blob/master/press/api/site.py)
- [SOURCE CODE] Injection when an app is installed on a live site: `Site.install_marketplace_conf(app, plan)` creates the subscription and then `self.update_site_config({f"sk_{subscription.document_name}": subscription.secret_key})`. The inline comment (present at HEAD 2026-10-20): "Marketplace apps authenticate to the Marketplace Developer API with a per-site secret read from their own site_config as `sk_<app>`. ### Inferences
- Recommended pattern (synthesised from the three real implementations above):
  1. `key = frappe.conf.get(f"sk_{app_name}")`; if falsy, treat as self-hosted / free tier (never crash).
  2. POST `{"secret_key": key}` to `https://frappecloud.com/api/method/press.api.developer.marketplace.get_subscription_info` with a short timeout; read `message.plan` (the plan's **name**, e.g. the Marketplace App Plan docname/title) and `message.enabled`.
  3. Cache the result (e.g. `frappe.cache` with a TTL of hours) and use a scheduler job to refresh, so plan checks never block requests; fail open on network errors (as easy_cash does) or fail closed after a grace period if the business requires.
  4. Gate features by plan name/limits in Python (server side), not in JS.
  5. Optionally expose a `subscription_update_hook` receiver, but note press only fires it for erpnext/hrms today.
- `get_subscription_status` returns a bare status string ("Active"/"Inactive"/"Disabled"-style) while `get_subscription_info` also returns `plan`; use the latter for tiered plans.
- Keys named `app_plans` / `marketplace_app_plans` are **press-side** (Site doctype field `app_plans`, dashboard `selected_app_plans`), not site-config keys; nothing in the codebase writes a plan name into the customer's `site_config.json`.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the exact string values of `Subscription.status` returned by `get_subscription_status` (field options not inspected; HRMS compares the message to a truthy check only).
- No official developer-facing documentation page for the `press.api.developer.marketplace` endpoints was found (the Frappe Cloud docs hosts were unreachable; search surfaced none), so this API is documented only by source code and by example apps.
- Whether Frappe Cloud v2 / new-registry apps receive `sk_<app>` the same way is unverified.

## Key Question 3: How do vendors sell to self-hosted customers and prevent piracy pragmatically?

### Takeaway
There is no official Frappe license-key mechanism for self-hosted sites; the marketplace secret only exists on Frappe Cloud. Vendors in practice (a) keep the code open source and sell support/hosting/implementation, (b) sell the app outside the marketplace as a one-off or annual fee with no technical enforcement, or (c) check `sk_<app>` on Frappe Cloud and simply allow unlimited use when self-hosted. No reliable example of a hard license-key server for a Frappe app was found.

### Cited Findings
- [COMMUNITY] Self-hosted users install marketplace apps by hand: "you have to use bench get-app to get it from its repository, then bench install-app xxxxx... then bench build, and bench restart." — [Marketplace Apps for self hosting (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/marketplace-apps-for-self-hosting/91010)
- [REAL-WORLD CODE] easy_cash's explicit self-hosted branch: when there is no `sk_` key and `on_frappecloud()` is false the plan is `None` -> displayed as "Self-Hosted" with "Unlimited" limits; upsell is a WhatsApp number in the error message. — [easy_cash/api/plan.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ahmedyousef96/erpnext_easy_cash/main/easy_cash/api/plan.py)
- [REAL-WORLD CODE] Frappe HR restricts SaaS-only behaviour by hostname: `if not frappe.utils.get_url().endswith(".frappehr.com"): return` — i.e. paid-SaaS logic is gated to Frappe's own domain, and self-hosted installs get the full GPL app. — [hrms/subscription_utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frappe/hrms/develop/hrms/subscription_utils.py)
- [VENDOR SITE via snippet] Webkul sells "ERPNext PowerBI Connector" for USD 399 (single-application license) plus a USD 79.80 installation add-on, listed for ERPNext v15 and updated Jan 2026 — a one-off purchase outside the Frappe marketplace, with no stated technical enforcement. — [Webkul store](https://store.webkul.com/erpnext-powerbi-connector.html)
- [VENDOR SITES via snippet] ZATCA (Saudi e-invoicing) apps: ERPGulf's is "License MIT" on GitHub and works "on Frappe Cloud or self-hosted servers"; Beveren's ZATCA_Integration is AGPL-3.0 on GitHub while its Frappe Cloud listing is a paid app. — [ERPGulf/zatca_erpgulf](https://github.com/ERPGulf/zatca_erpgulf); [Beveren ZATCA_Integration](https://github.com/Beveren-Software-Inc/ZATCA_Integration); [Beveren listing](https://cloud.frappe.io/marketplace/apps/zatca_integration)
- [COMMUNITY] Forum question (Sept 2026): can a paid marketplace app be non-open-source? The poster "found no marketplace app without" a public repo; no authoritative answer in the snippet. — [Paid Market App (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/paid-market-app/164874)
- [COMMUNITY] Security advice relevant to key handling: "API tokens and all client tokens are always visible to the client", recommend "a separate server to make requests on the client's behalf". — [Api secret and Api key (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/api-secret-and-api-key/83863)
- [SEARCH RESULT] A GitHub search for Frappe apps implementing license-key validation/activation returned no reference implementation. — [search summary](https://github.com/frappe/press)

### Inferences
- Because every marketplace app must be MIT/GPL-compatible and hosted on a public GitHub repo, "piracy prevention" in the Frappe ecosystem is mostly social/commercial: paying customers get Frappe Cloud one-click install + updates + support; self-hosters can run the code freely. Vendors price the *service* (support tickets, training, hosting), as POS Awesome's tiers show (see Q4).
- A pragmatic design for a dashboard/charts app: ship the Frappe app open source (MIT), keep any genuinely proprietary value (e.g. hosted rendering, premium chart packs, AI features) behind a vendor API that requires a vendor-issued key for self-hosted customers, and on Frappe Cloud accept `sk_<app>` via `get_subscription_info`. This is an inference; no vendor doing exactly this was found.
- Vendors who want a classic license key for self-hosted customers must build their own license server; frappe provides `frappe.utils.frappecloud.on_frappecloud()` to branch behaviour but nothing for licensing.

### Gaps
- No vendor's self-hosted license-key implementation or pricing terms (per site / per year) could be verified; Webkul's price is the only concrete self-hosted paid add-on figure found.
- Could not read the full forum threads (hosts blocked), so Frappe staff replies on the "is open source mandatory for paid apps" question were not captured.

## Key Question 4: Price points of comparable paid apps (dashboard / BI / reporting / charts especially)

### Takeaway
Confirmed marketplace price points are sparse: POS Awesome lists USD 50 / 100 / 200 per site per month (tiers differ by support, not features); Frappe's own Insights is free/open source with Frappe Cloud hosting tiers from ~USD 5 to 38 per site per month (third-party listing); outside the marketplace, Webkul's Power BI connector is USD 399 one-off and Peliqan's managed BI pipeline starts at USD 380/month. No paid charts/dashboard app on the Frappe marketplace with a published price was found.

## Key Question 5: Can a proprietary licence coexist with Frappe's ecosystem; what do vendors actually do?

### Takeaway
Legally, proprietary apps on the MIT-licensed Frappe Framework are possible, but anything depending on ERPNext (GPL-3.0) or the newer Frappe apps (AGPL-3.0, e.g. CRM, Helpdesk, Insights) is contested, and the Frappe Cloud Marketplace flatly requires MIT/GPL-compatible open source for listing, including paid apps. In practice vendors keep the Frappe app open source and monetise via marketplace plans, support, hosting/SaaS, or external one-off sales; closed-source distribution happens outside the marketplace and is frowned upon by the community honour code.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL DOCS via snippet] "All apps on the Marketplace must be Open Source... MIT or compatible with the GNU General Public License"; non-compliant publishers are not accepted. — [Frappe Cloud Marketplace Overview](https://docs.frappe.io/cloud/marketplace)
- [OFFICIAL via snippet] "Frappe is distributed under the MIT license. ERPNext is distributed under the GPL-3.0" — both allow selling derived software, but GPL "requires you to also release your software under the GPL" (Frappe maintainer reply). — [Selling Apps built on frappe and Erpnext! (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/selling-apps-built-on-frappe-and-erpnext/78431)
- [OFFICIAL DOCS via snippet] "Newer Frappe applications (such as CRM, Helpdesk, etc) are released under the GNU Affero General Public License (AGPL)"; ERPNext name and logo are Frappe trademarks. — [Why open source? (Frappe customer guide)](https://docs.frappe.io/customer-guide/about-frappe/what-open-source-mean-for-you); [Attribution of Frappe's IP](https://frappe.io/blog/legal/protection-of-our-intellectual-property)
- [COMMUNITY] Honour-code thread: single-customer extensions need not be shared, but "generic extensions used by multiple customers should be shared" — explicitly "a community honour code, not a legal rule". — [Code of Conduct regarding Open vs Closed Source (forum)](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/imp-code-of-conduct-regarding-open-vs-closed-source/54055)

- [REAL-WORLD] Vendor behaviour observed: ERPGulf ZATCA = MIT on GitHub, revenue from implementation; Beveren ZATCA = AGPL on GitHub + paid marketplace plan; easy_cash = open GitHub repo + marketplace plan tiers + WhatsApp upsell; Webkul Power BI connector = sold off-marketplace at a fixed price. — [ERPGulf](https://github.com/ERPGulf/zatca_erpgulf); [Beveren](https://github.com/Beveren-Software-Inc/ZATCA_Integration); [easy_cash](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ahmedyousef96/erpnext_easy_cash/main/easy_cash/api/plan.py); [Webkul](https://store.webkul.com/erpnext-powerbi-connector.html)

### Inferences
- A charts/dashboard app that only depends on `frappe` (MIT) could legally be proprietary; the moment it imports ERPNext or an AGPL Frappe app it inherits copyleft risk, and in all cases it cannot be listed on the Frappe Cloud Marketplace unless open source. "Open-core" (MIT Frappe app + proprietary hosted service or JS bundle delivered from the vendor's CDN) is the coexistence pattern most consistent with both the rules and observed vendor behaviour, though no vendor was documented doing it explicitly.
- The ecosystem's commercial norm is "open code, paid convenience": Frappe Cloud one-click install, support tiers, implementation, and SaaS hosting.

### Gaps
- No legal opinion or Frappe policy page addressing "proprietary JS/compiled asset shipped in an MIT Frappe app" was found.
- Frappe staff replies in the 2026 threads (#164881, #164874, #164661) could not be read directly; positions above are from snippets and may omit nuance.
